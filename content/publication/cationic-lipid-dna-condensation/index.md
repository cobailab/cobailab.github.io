abstract: "The condensation of nucleic acids by lipids is a widespread phenomenon in biology with crucial implications for drug delivery. However, the mechanisms of DNA assembly in lipid bilayers remain insufficiently understood due to challenges in measuring and assessing each component’s contribution in the lipid–DNA–cation system. This study uses all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to investigate DNA condensation in cationic lipid bilayers. Our exhaustive exploration of the thermodynamic factors reveals unique roles for phospholipid head groups and cations. We observed that bridging cations between lipid and DNA drastically reduce charges, while mobile magnesium cations “ping-ponging” between double strands create charge fluctuations. While the first factor stabilizes the DNA–lipid complex, the latter creates attractive forces to induce the spontaneous condensation of DNAs. This novel mechanism not only sheds light on the current data regarding cationic lipid-induced DNA condensation but also provides potential design strategies for creating efficient gene delivery vectors for drug delivery."
